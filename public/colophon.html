Colophon

A colophon is the how, the why, the bones, the unseen and barely registered. It details the systems that built the space you see, explains some of the decisions that were made, and offers you a look under the hood so if you see something you like, you too can use it in your own space.


last updated November 26, 2026

...but WHY?

As I get older I find myself becoming more and more nostalgic (which is saying something for a guy who has kept every postcard and christmas card he has recived for the last multiple decades), longing for a time and place that simply does not exist. I was born in the late 1900's as the kids would say today and was #blessed to grow up in the 90's and come of age in the early Aughts. We had it all, and the internet (or World Wide Web, or Net as we called it) felt like a real community where you could find your corner, with your people, and be the weirdo you wanted to be. Or anybody you wanted to be for that matter. This is my attempt to recreate some of that for myself, and anyone else who wants to join me.

Heavily influenced by my reading on the IndiWeb. As I learn, I am figuring out what my place may be in this community, and doing the work to ensure my site is in practice what I say it is on paper. Transparency is the name of the game, fitting squarely in line with my guiding principals of *Learning in Public* and building *authentic, collaborative community*.

What Makes Her Purrrrr

HTML5, CSS3, and vanilla JavaScript. Initial code written by Calude Code with my ideas and vision. The page launches with no frameworks, no build step, no npm install, the way the web gods intended.

The "Hit Counter" is a tiny Cloudflare Pages Function that asks Cloudflare how many visits the alley got in the last 30 days, so the counter on the homepage shows a real number (rounded up to the next hundred place).

If you want to see the code for yourself, go to this Github repository, and take a look under the hood. In line with an open source mentality and philosophy, there is no associated license with any part of the main alley.

Where The Alley Couchsurfs

Hosted through Cloudflare using Cloudflare Pages. Cloudflare also runs all of the DNS. When I make an update to the code (using VSCode or Github web) and Github recieves the change, the content is published right then and there. Cool in that there are not a million steps to take edits live, humbling because it means I will publish a lot of errors. Y'all be nice to me, or I'll cry. Email runs through my personal domain but is handled through Proton with all the encryption and privacy bells and whistles you could want as an everyday person.

Skynet's Metal Arm Helped Get Us Here

Claude Code is the source of the day 1 code after I laid out the vision and wacky ideas. I then am responsible for making changes, fixing things that I broke, and doing a heavy amount of experiential learning with Claude as an instructor when times get tough.

Claude also created a lot of the copy that you currently see on the site as a placeholder for me to go back and enter my own words inito the spaces he is keeping warm. An official /ai slash page is in development right now and will be available poste haste.

Neon, Hypercolor, Oh My!

I wanted to recreate some of my favorite visual elements that I remember from my childhood as I could (while understanding I don't know a damn thing and not knowing what potential existed; or limits for that matter). In its current state we are working with a lot of neon. The neon bar signs with their tubey, overly bright greatness still have homes on the walls of my home. In addition visions of spray paint graffitti, the zig zag carpet at the roller rink-arcade combo. So. Many. Colors.

The sign over the entrance reads Fluharty Alley, the flickering glow beckoning you to come see what fun and interesting places await you. Neon signs as page titles just made sense to me. The favicon bear is the unofficial mascot of all this, the reprsentation of a holdover from my last web venture centered around bear wordplay (if you see the handle/phrase "Bearly in a Hurry", it is because of that miguided project) and was fully created by Claude as another placeholder element.

The Fancy Squiggles

Tilt Neon and Neonderthaw for the neon tubes, Rubik Dirt for the posters, Permanent Marker for the tags, and Work Sans for everything you actually have to read.

All of these fonts load from this website itself, so your system doesnt have to wake somebody else up in the middle of the night so you can see the different font types. Permanent Marker is under the Apache License 2.0; the rest are under the SIL Open Font License.

Neighborhood Watch

No narcs live in this cul-de-sac. You are not tracked or followed by anything on this site. No cookies, no ads, no trackers. Visits are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which doesn't use cookies in its process. The full picture with more detail and fuller coverage can be found by reading the privacy policy.

Opening the Doors As Wide As Possible

The alley is wide enough to fit everyone between its walls. I am admitedly still learning about all the ways the internet and personal pages can be more accessible to folks. My intenetion is never to create anything that can't be consumed and enjoyed by the majority of people who come through this space (Except the ghost of Ronald Regan, he isn't welcome here, ever) Every animation on this site respects prefers-reduced-motion, everything works from a keyboard, and the markup is semantic HTML underneath all the spray paint fumes and glowing neon.

Every page also has a "Skip to content" link for keyboard and screen reader users. It stays hidden until you need it: press Tab when a page first loads and it pops up in the top corner.

If you have an accessibility need that is not being addressed, have a suggestion for an improvement that will allow access to more people, or make anything easier to access for anyone, PLEASE reach out to me through any of the (what feels like) hundreds of ways I have listed around this place to contact me, and I will make it happen.

Heavy is ths Crown

These pages clock in at around 400 KB (Jenny Craig signed off on their check ins), and about half of that is the grit the developers baked into Rubik Dirt (Totally worth it, the font is bitchin' y'all). No video, no trackers, no megabytes of JavaScript (For now...).

Our Quirky Friends and Community

The homepage carries an h-card and rel="me" links, this allows me to be verified as a real and genuine human who am who I say I am (grammar is hard) and I am from this Alley. The alley is also on the IndieWeb Webring; (remember webrings? They were so cooooooool!) clicking the arrows at the bottom of the page will take you on a hop to my neighbors in the ring (tell them I said hi when you get there). The now page is the gift of an idea given to the world by Derek Sivers, and the very page you are reading all this amazing content was first introduced to me here, the IndiWeb wiki on a colophon.

I couldn't make a website without bringing back one of my FAVORITE elements of the old web that you just don't see anymore, a links page. : a blogroll of gardens and personal sites I like, an OPML file of it for feed readers, and an 88x31 button for anyone who wants to link back.

The now page has its own Atom feed, so a feed reader can tell you when there's news from the road. It's built straight from the page itself, so it never falls behind.

The living, growing stuff (notes, essays, half-formed ideas) lives in the digital garden, powered by Obsidian. This site is just the front door.
